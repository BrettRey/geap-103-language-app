# English (Canada)

language: English
language code: en-CA
made by: Brett
for: newcomers who want everyday phrases for life in Toronto

> This is the example pack. Your pack teaches your own language, so your
> phrases will be in your language and your meanings will be in English.

## Saying hello

phrase: How's it going?
meaning: Hello. How are you?
say it: howz it GO-ing
note: This is a greeting, not a real question. A short answer is polite, like "Good, you?"

phrase: Not bad, you?
meaning: I'm OK. How are you?
say it: not BAD, YOU
note: "You?" is short for "How about you?"

phrase: Nice to meet you.
meaning: I'm happy to meet you for the first time.
say it: NICE tuh MEET-cha
note: Say this the first time you meet someone.

phrase: See you later.
meaning: Goodbye.
say it: SEE-ya LAY-ter
note: You can say this even if you won't see the person later. It just means goodbye.

## Being polite

phrase: Sorry!
meaning: Excuse me. / Oops.
say it: SOR-ee
note: Canadians say "sorry" a lot, even for small things, like almost bumping into someone. It doesn't always mean "It was my fault."

phrase: No worries.
meaning: It's OK. / You're welcome.
say it: no WUR-eez
note: A friendly answer to "Sorry" or "Thank you."

phrase: Is this seat taken?
meaning: Can I sit here?
say it: iz this SEET TAY-ken
note: Use it on the bus, in class or in a café. If someone says "No," the seat is free.

phrase: Could you say that again, please?
meaning: Please repeat that.
say it: COOD-ja say that uh-GEN, pleez
note: This is polite and normal. Most people are happy to repeat.

## At a coffee shop

phrase: Can I get a medium coffee, please?
meaning: I'd like a medium coffee.
say it: can-eye GET uh MEE-dee-um COF-ee, pleez
note: "Can I get ...?" is a normal, polite way to order food or drinks.

phrase: For here or to go?
meaning: Will you have it here, or take it with you?
say it: for HEER or tuh GO
note: The server asks this. Answer "For here" or "To go."

phrase: A double-double, please.
meaning: A coffee with two creams and two sugars, please.
say it: uh DUB-ul DUB-ul, pleez
note: You'll hear this at Tim Hortons, a Canadian coffee chain.

phrase: Can I pay with debit?
meaning: Can I pay with my bank card?
say it: can-eye PAY with DEB-it
note: "Debit" means the money comes straight from your bank account.
